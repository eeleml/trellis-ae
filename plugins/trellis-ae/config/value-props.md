# Trellis Value Propositions (generic — safe to ship publicly)

Pick ONE primary value prop per contact based on research. No customer names or metrics live here —
the proof points (case studies) live in the local baked index `config/case-studies.md` (gitignored,
zip-baked; the Drive index is the source of truth for updating it).

## When to use each
### Ads Automation
**Use when:** actively running Amazon ads, ACOS/efficiency pain, performance-marketing background,
or a competitive ad-heavy category. **Hook:** wasted ad spend, scaling without degrading ACOS.

### Dynamic Pricing
**Use when:** highly competitive/commodity category, price-sensitive buyers, many direct competitors,
or pricing named as a challenge. **Hook:** losing the Buy Box, reactive pricing, undercutting.

### Fully-Managed Service
**Use when:** founder/operator-led, small team, overwhelmed with Amazon ops, wants to focus on
product/brand/DTC. **Hook:** bandwidth, operational complexity, "just want to focus on the product."

### Qore (the operating layer)
*(Naming: the product is **Qore**; "Trellis" is the parent company, never a thing you buy. Never "Core,"
"Core by Trellis," or "Amplify" — dead names. Source: Qore Sales Training in Notion, 01/02/04.)*
**What it is (for the pitch):** the transparent operating layer for Amazon ad teams — it turns the
playbook the team runs by hand (weekly audits, reporting, SOP checks, wasted-spend sweeps) into scheduled
workflows that run the same way every time, fully auditable, with sign-off before anything changes
in-market. Not another dashboard, not a black box. "Not to replace the team — to raise the floor."
**Use when (either door — brands AND agencies):** a real ads team/operator (in-house brand team, or an
agency running client accounts); key-person risk ("the account knowledge is one person deep," "the whole
channel routes through one person"); hours of manual reporting/audits every week; the team re-answers the
same questions; already tinkering with ChatGPT/Claude and hitting inconsistency; multi-channel ambitions
(Walmart / TikTok / Google / Shopify alongside Amazon). Also the natural closed-lost angle when the deal
was lost on price/budget or "wanted to keep it in-house" — a lighter way in than the managed motion.
**Hooks that land (pick by seat):**
- **Brand-side:** the whole Amazon channel routes through you · your team re-answers the same question
  every week · hard to defend a spend decision when the tool can't show its reasoning.
- **Agency-side:** your best accounts are one person deep · catch PPC issues before your clients do · the
  SOPs you keep writing should run, not sit in a doc.
**Skip it when:** Amazon ads is a side task with no dedicated owner (no playbook to run), or they only
want a Buy-Box repricer (that's Dynamic Pricing — route honestly).
**Qore copy rules:** **no prices and no commercial terms in emails** — no dollar figures, tiers, or terms
like "month-to-month"/beta terms; commercials are changing often, so the rep handles them live (a reply or
a call), never in drafted copy. **Proof = ONE short beta-customer quote, no numbers** (e.g. "it did the
analysis that took me two weeks in five minutes") — no beta counts or stats, and never attribute the
ads/pricing case-study metrics to Qore. Outcome-led, never tech-led; the no-AI-mention rule applies to
Qore too ("runs your playbook the same way every time," not "AI").

## Agencies: ICP only if they manage Amazon ads/pricing for clients
An Amazon / e-commerce agency is **not automatically a competitor, but not automatically ICP either** — fit
depends on what they actually do for clients on Amazon. Trellis is the platform an agency would *run* on, so
the test is whether their work overlaps what Trellis does (ads + pricing):
- **ICP** — agencies that **manage ad spend (and/or pricing) on Amazon** for their client brands. Trellis lets
  them run ads/pricing across their whole book: scale client results without scaling headcount, save hours,
  white-label. **Never disqualify one of these just for being an agency.**
- **Not ICP** — agencies whose Amazon work is **creative, content/photography, listing design,
  fulfillment/logistics, or other non-ads/pricing services**. Trellis's product doesn't map to what they
  sell, so they're not a fit (don't pitch them).
- **Maybe — flag it** — agencies that do ads/pricing **and** other services. They can be ICP on the ads/pricing
  side, but call it out so the AE knows it's a partial fit and can confirm the relevant service line is real.

If research can't confirm an agency manages Amazon ads/pricing, treat the fit as **unconfirmed and flag it**,
don't assume ICP.

**Angle (for the ICP ones):** manage pricing + ads across all your brands from one place; grow client results
without growing hours. **Proof:** the agency case studies (e.g., Sequence Commerce, 25+ brands; World
Products, ~1,650 hours saved). Value prop is usually Ads Automation and/or Dynamic Pricing at portfolio
scale, or Fully-Managed for a smaller shop — or **Qore** when the signal is standards/consistency across
accounts ("your best accounts are one person deep," "catch issues before your clients do").

## Vertical → value-prop affinity (starting point, not a rule)
| Vertical | Primary | Secondary |
|----------|---------|-----------|
| Supplements | Ads Automation | Fully-Managed |
| Beauty/Skincare | Ads Automation | Dynamic Pricing |
| Apparel | Dynamic Pricing | Ads Automation |
| Home Goods | Fully-Managed | Dynamic Pricing |
| Electronics | Dynamic Pricing | Ads Automation |
| Food/Beverage | Ads Automation | Fully-Managed |
| Pet | Ads Automation | Dynamic Pricing |
| Sports/Outdoors | Dynamic Pricing | Fully-Managed |

**Qore is buyer-type-led, not vertical-led** — it doesn't slot into the vertical table. Pick it on the
*how-they-operate* signal (a real ads team/operator, key-person risk, SOP/reporting grind, already
tinkering with ChatGPT/Claude, multi-channel), whatever the vertical. When the signal is ads efficiency
or Buy Box specifically, Ads/Pricing still lead; Qore leads when the signal is how the team *runs* the
account, not the account's performance.
